How I lead design

The products above came from teams, not from one person. This is how I build and run them. The full story is in the leadership case.

Read the full story

I’ve hired and onboarded 12 designers onto client projects. At its largest my team was 6 designers reporting directly to me, on up to 4 products at the same time.

Every year I set goals with each designer: where they wanted to grow and where the gaps were. I wrote situational tests to find those gaps, and we tracked reviews and strengths in Valor’s internal review tool. Two designers grew to the next level: junior to mid, and mid to senior.

I build and run design systems: audit what exists, consolidate it, set contribution rules and get teams to adopt them. I built Mabrook’s system for 16+ verticals, led SAP’s design system pilot and helped build Cosmos at ASI Central (two brands, one system). Today a token-based system is also the context our AI agents design from.

I base design decisions on evidence: 70+ user and stakeholder interviews, hypotheses, usability testing and product analytics. Most cases on this page have a metric because the team agreed on it before designing.

I built AI into how the team works, not only into its tools: Claude skills and agents for recurring work, a token-based design system as their context, and handoff as a clickable prototype in code. Results: Mabrook feature release from 10 to 4 days, ASI Central brief to prototype from 11 to 3.5 days.

The team uses Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, Codex and Figma AI, with Claude and Codex connected to Figma. Figma stays alongside the code: the place where every role reviews and comments, and a safe parallel copy of every design artifact.
